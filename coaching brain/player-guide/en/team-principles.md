## Calls

**Share state, don't give orders** `S1–S6`
▶ Say what you're doing: ✅ *"I'm at mid, pushing computer."* ✅ *"I have mid."* ✖ *"Go computer!"* (an order). Telling the team **where you're going first** ("I'm going green side") lets teammates decide to follow — and the team has to build a culture of answering.

**Agree outside the game; call in one phrase** `S10`
▶ A plan agreed outside the game (e.g. Raid defense → push the spawn, red side first) needs just one intention call: *"I take red side."* The coach's biggest frustration: plans made outside the game that nobody tells the team about in the game. ✖ An order like "go flank" takes away the player's own judgment.

**Promise system** `S6`
▶ Agree *who, when, what* beforehand, then only execute in game. Example: **operator popped → I jump in** (sacrifice). Calling "no playing scared, sacrifice!" in the middle of the game is too late — agree before.

**After a wipe, a direction call is a must** `S8`
▶ You don't need a call every life, but **after the whole team dies there must be a direction call** — AR or anyone. Knowing the right answer isn't enough; the team that executes first wins.

**If you say it, mean it** `S9`
▶ Calling "I have a War Machine" **once** is enough. If you say "I'll use it," **you must use it** — the team changes its play around it. If you don't, trust drops and teammates start checking your screen themselves (delay). Say it even if you think it may fail. Call your movements too (e.g. pushing toward the bridge) so a teammate can act differently.

**Callouts have two faces** `S6`
▶ Enemy position info phases out in seconds. Too many callouts hurt your own gunfight focus. After 2 kills, **move on purpose** — the enemy knows where you are. **Trade-kill value comes before holding a position.**

**Say what you're doing — a statement, not an order** `S18`
▶ *"You don't have to dictate. Just let your teammate know what you're doing."* Examples: *"I'll contest"* with 20 s left; *"I'm going P2 side"* so teammates inside put firepower there; an SMG says *"I'm pushing out"* or *"I'm backing off"*; a rat play says *"I'm watching this area."* You don't have to give orders, the full plan, or talk about every moment; teammates don't have to follow — what teammates do next is theirs. **The coach's answer to the players' worries** (calls get ignored; at a high level it's too late; "I'll stop calling"): mandatory calls (break, rotation) are already agreed; beyond that, share only information **you** think is important, and when the team doesn't know (e.g. a contest with 20 s left).

## Roles and structure

**Don't cling to a position** `S6`
▶ Catwalk isn't a comfortable spot for any weapon role and is not mandatory; cycling is enough. **Clinging to a position = playing not to lose.** Moving with the team matters more.

**Technical misses are structure, not ability** `S5 · S6`
▶ An equalizer that wasn't used was a HUD button the player couldn't see (fixed in the HUD); guessing before using a War Machine was a habit (fixed with "see, then pop"). Fix the structure/habit/routine first.

**Talk about behavior as rules, not about people** `S2`
▶ When two players clash, the cause is usually that nobody agreed "who does what" — write roles and rules down.
