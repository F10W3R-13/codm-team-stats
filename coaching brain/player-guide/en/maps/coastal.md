## Defense

### A) A is easy to flee, B is hard `S19 · Oct 30` ✅

**Situation:** You defend Coastal B.

▶ **Do:** *"In Coastal B, on defense, you either play safe or you explore and share whatever you have with these guys."* On A lane you can peek long and then **exit safely through the staircase**, so you needn't be extremely proactive and you get more valuable info safely. On B, you only flee after the enemy is already in the site. To get value from B: **(1)** take a spot and **keep your life**, or **(2)** explore **one corner at a time** and **tell the team** you're ready. ("That's a map thing.")

✖ **Don't:** Treat A and B the same way.

### B) Talk between sites; use mid `S19` ✅

**Situation:** You defend in a split (two on A, two on B).

▶ **Do:** **Communication between the two sites is the most important thing now.** Holding mid gives you freedom: to clear A cut you can use mid to wash restaurant / cafe / grocery on the A side, and then pick up corners toward **A bar side.** For that, mid players must know **how ready** the others are. A bar is a straight corridor, not hard. **On B, if two are inside at the start, you may explore B short a little — one step at a time, with the team knowing.** The reason to say it isn't duty; it's the signal that you're ready to help the mid side.

*Example:* if the B-side player had actively cleared the empty B side early, the A side would have learned much sooner that an enemy was at A bar, and could have cleared A cut before being slammed through A long. You can push A short decisively without info, but it's risky — you need mid's help.

✖ **Don't:** Explore silently — the info does nothing for the team if no one hears it.

### C) Alone at B: always have an escape route `S19` ✅

**Situation:** Everyone stacks B and you're alone at the site.

▶ **Do:** **Always have a route to run from what happens in the site.**
