## Attack

### A) Map control, not a forced lane `S8` ✅

**Situation:** You attack in Control. Example: a round lost despite a 12-kill, 0-death start — an *operations* problem, not a kill problem.

▶ **Do:** Focus on **map control.** Secure **B site (easier than A) and mid at the same time**, don't over-extend, and widen the ticket gap with **trade kills.** With about a **2× ticket lead (30 vs 15)**, don't over-commit: build an 8–16 / 10–20 gap, *then* move. The sniper's essence is **suppression** — the side with one-shot potential makes the other side's mistakes; if the enemy peeks, they're wrong.

✖ **Don't:** Force one lane. Spawn-kill for extra value (low value, more risk). Push kitchen alone with the sniper.

### B) Beached at the mid stairs `S8` ✅

**Situation:** The team stayed at P5 → mid stairs for almost a minute and gave the A lane away.

▶ **Do:** Frame it: staying = **low risk / low gain**, pushing up = **high risk / high gain.** The options at that moment were: push kitchen together, push the A lane together, or hold mid. The mid stairs are **not a power position** — fighting from rock is better.

✖ **Don't:** Sit too long. Don't pick the **A lane** because of one kill (the enemy had head glitch / bar — a perfect setup, and we had no advantage — "the worst choice"). Don't ignore a lone enemy at apps for 30+ s without pushing — it is cumulative loss. Locking spawn with the long block doesn't help if you don't have the A lane (the enemy gets a neutral spawn).

*Comeback loss 13–16:* after a player died, the team made no decision and stayed in the safe zone, and **solo deaths** piled up — that was fatal.

## Defense

### A) Lane control first, mid is a hub `S17 · Oct 21` ✅

**Situation:** You defend Raid (the coach said it directly: "the advantage of the defensive side in Raid").

▶ **Do:**
- **Smoke the outer lane** (alley) to worry less about it, and keep **more people at mid.** Mid is the **hub** to send players to B, A and the mid stairs.
- **Don't rush when the enemy takes mid / P5.** Even if you're pushed to the pillar you can suppress, block the kitchen corner and use high ground. Absorb P5 like a **sponge** and farm kills in your preferred zones (corners, head glitches). The enemy will eventually move out — wait for it.
- If the enemy takes mid, **split lanes and pinch from all sides.** When a real chance to retake P5 comes, take it — but if they're already settled in, don't hurry.
- **A site:** smoke A to isolate it (they'll try to isolate you). Unless they put **4 on A**, 1–2 on B is fine. You don't need force-to-force — that's the defender's edge in Raid; use it.
- **One thing to protect:** don't give the kitchen side to the enemy.
- **One-line summary:** *"When you're defense, lane control is more important. Don't try to rush yourself just because you want to retake a certain point or certain area, which can be P5 or A or B. Control the lane first. Try to farm some kills and then you can just move on."*

✖ **Don't:** Stack numbers on one site. Rush to retake a point just because you want it.

### B) The "I'll kill him" call `S17` ✅

**Situation:** You know where an enemy is while capturing B.

▶ **Do:** **Call "I'll kill him"** — good, teammates can help — but **once you say it, finish it.** **Hunt the one enemy whose position you know** before pushing a lane, whatever the capture state; if you leave that enemy alone, you don't know what value they bring after capturing B. Hunting also makes you physically see what your teammates are doing.

✖ **Don't:** Everyone looking in a different place (one at mid, one at A, one at spawn).

### C) Defense priority: spawn push `S10 · Oct 10` ✅

**Situation:** You have an opening to push the enemy spawn.

▶ **Do:** Push the spawn whenever there's a chance — **take red side first**, or **return to the spawn via the water route.** This plan was already agreed outside the game, so a single call — *"I take red side"* — should be understood. **The highest-leverage player is the one who can get kills at spawn** (they take the most tickets): the others stay close to that player — don't react to calls of teammates dying at mid. An isolated pusher needs help **and a heads-up.** An isolated player plays safe.

✖ **Don't:** Think only "how do I get a kill?" (e.g. "double stairs are clear, hide in a corner for a pick"). Makes sense alone, means nothing as a team: the enemy stays **close together**, turns our solo plays into numbers and gets trades; ours didn't. At **10 points behind**, the coach's view: the priority is **life preservation and crossfire**, not "go flank" backstabs — let the player decide in their own style rather than give a one-line order.
